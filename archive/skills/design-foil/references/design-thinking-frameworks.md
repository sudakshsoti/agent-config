# Design-thinking frameworks

Load this on demand during Brainstorm mode. Pick the framework whose question matches the moment; don't force-fit one just because it's here. Most conversations only need one or two.

## Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)

**Question it answers:** what is the user actually "hiring" this product to do?
**Reach for this when:** the conversation is stuck describing features instead of the underlying motivation, or a feature request feels arbitrary until you ask what job it's standing in for.

## Business Model Canvas (BMC)

**Question it answers:** how does the whole business fit together, spanning value proposition, channels, revenue streams, cost structure, and key partners?
**Reach for this when:** you need the full picture fast, especially in the standing "reason out the industry" step, before critiquing a single flow in isolation.

## Value Proposition Canvas (VPC)

**Question it answers:** does what we offer (pain relievers, gain creators, products/services) actually match what the customer needs (pains, gains, jobs)?
**Reach for this when:** BMC gave you the business shape but the fit between offer and customer is still fuzzy, or a feature feels like a solution in search of a pain.

## Double Diamond

**Question it answers:** are we diverging or converging right now, and on the problem or the solution?
**Reach for this when:** the conversation is jumping straight to solutions without agreeing on the problem, or ideation has gone wide and needs a converge step before it's useful.

## Service Design Blueprint

**Question it answers:** what has to happen backstage (systems, ops, other teams) to support the frontstage experience the user sees?
**Reach for this when:** a UX idea sounds great but its operational dependencies haven't been named, or a flow's failure points are actually in handoffs between people/systems, not screens.

## Kano Model

**Question it answers:** is this feature a basic expectation, a linear satisfier, or a delighter, and does it deserve the effort being proposed for it?
**Reach for this when:** prioritisation is stuck on "how much polish does this deserve," or a team is over-investing in a feature that's table stakes and under-investing in the thing that would actually delight.

## Wardley Mapping

**Question it answers:** where does each component of the system sit on the evolution axis from genesis to commodity, and what does that imply about build vs. buy vs. differentiate?
**Reach for this when:** a build-vs-buy or roadmap-investment argument needs a visual anchor, or the team is polishing something that should be commoditised (or vice versa).

## North Star Framework

**Question it answers:** what single metric captures the value we deliver to customers, and what inputs actually move it?
**Reach for this when:** a roadmap has too many disconnected goals, or a feature's success metric is a vanity number disconnected from real value delivered.

## Opportunity Solution Tree

**Question it answers:** which opportunities (pains, needs) actually ladder up to the desired outcome, and which solutions genuinely address a given opportunity?
**Reach for this when:** a backlog is a flat list of ideas with no visible link back to outcomes, or two competing solutions need to be evaluated against the same opportunity rather than against each other directly.

## RICE / Impact-Effort

**Question it answers:** given a pile of candidate ideas, which are worth doing first?
**Reach for this when:** brainstorming has produced more ideas than anyone can act on and the conversation needs to move from "what could we do" to "what should we do next," fast and defensibly.
