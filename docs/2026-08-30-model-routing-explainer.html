Which agent runs on which model, and why

Every model choice in omp/config.yml, explained. The short version: OpenCode Go does not bill you in dollars, it bills you in percentages of each model's own allowance, and that single fact decides the whole layout.

Written on the day the monthly window hit 100% with three days left, after a single code review made 542 model calls in fourteen minutes. The fix was not spending less. It was spending on models with bigger allowances.

The monthly limit is a sum of percentages, not a pile of money

Each model on OpenCode Go carries its own monthly dollar allowance, either $15, $30 or $60 depending on tier. The plan's "Monthly limit" bar is not the total dollars you spent. It is the sum of how much of each model's own allowance you used, capped at 100%.

The dashboard read 102.6% on 30 August. Add up the percentage column and you get exactly that, which is how the mechanism was confirmed:

GPT 5.6 Luna 55.1 Hy3 10.3 GLM 5.3 9.4 Grok 4.6 8.0 DeepSeek V4 Pro 4.2 Kimi K3 3.8 Grok 4.5 3.3 Muse Spark 1.2 2.2 Qwen3.8 Max 1.9 Hy4 preview 1.5 MiniMax M3 1.3 DeepSeek V4 Flash 1.2 GLM 5.3 Flash 0.4 ───── 102.6 ← the "Monthly 100%" wall

So the number to keep small is percentage points, not rupees or dollars. And the exchange rate between them depends entirely on which model you picked. One dollar spent on a $60-allowance model costs 1.67 points. The same dollar on a $15-allowance model costs 6.67 points. Four times the plan cost for identical spend.

Picked for allowance size first, price second. All three sit on large allowances and are barely touched, which is why they can absorb the entire fleet.

Those three add up to 3.8% of the month. You could spend twenty five times more on them and still fit inside the plan. That is the entire argument for this layout.

The last column is worth staring at. omp priced GLM 5.3 at $11.36 and Luna at $2.67, ranking GLM as four times the bigger spender. The plan says the reverse: Luna took 55.1% of the month against GLM 5.3's 9.4%, nearly six times more. More on that in the corrections below.

One caveat on the allowance figures. The percentages are read straight off the dashboard. The $15/$30/$60 tiers come from OpenCode's published pricing notes, and the dashboard's own dollar readings hint the real tiers may be larger. Routing does not depend on which is right: the ratio between the top and bottom tier is 4x either way, and the ratio is the only thing that decides where work should go.

Why cache reads decide the reviewer, not output price

A code reviewer re-reads the same diff on every one of its turns. One review on 30 August made 542 subagent calls and sent 28.68M cache-read tokens against only 1.39M fresh input and 445K output. Cache reads were 95% of the traffic.

So for this role the cache-read rate is the only rate that matters, and it is the one nobody looks at:

glm-5.3                      cache reads $0.26 /Mtok
muse-spark-1.2-contributor   cache reads $0.002/Mtok   130x cheaper

Muse Spark wins twice over. Lowest cache-read rate in the fleet, and the largest allowance to spend it against.

What happens when a model refuses

A fallback chain is the ordered list omp walks when a call fails. Chains are matched by how specific the key is: an exact provider/model-id beats a provider/* wildcard, which beats the role's own chain, which beats default.

exact keyopencode-go/muse-spark-1.2-contributor
  1. 1deepseek-v4-flash $30 allowance, 1.2% used
  2. 2glm-5.3-flash $30 allowance, 0.4% used
  3. 3claude-sonnet-5 Max quota, always answers
exact key, no Claude allowedopencode-go/glm-5.3-flash
  1. 1deepseek-v4-flash DeepSeek family
  2. 2muse-spark-1.2-contributor Meta family
wildcardopencode-go/*
  1. 1muse-spark-1.2-contributor biggest allowance first
  2. 2claude-sonnet-5
wildcardanthropic/*
  1. 1muse-spark-1.2-contributor
  2. 2glm-5.3-flash
  3. 3deepseek-v4-flash no Claude rung, on purpose

Two rules are built into those lists and both are easy to undo by accident.

  • A failed Claude call never retries on Claude. If Anthropic is what broke, sending the retry back to Anthropic wastes it. That is why anthropic/* has no Claude rung.
  • The adversary never lands on Claude. Its whole job is to critique a plan Claude wrote, from outside Claude's blind spots. It gets its own exact-model key so the wildcard's Claude rung cannot catch it.
The second rule has a hole worth knowing. If every rung in a chain is unavailable, omp does not fail the call. It falls through to the default chain, which ends on Sonnet so that something always answers. This was observed on 30 August: with OpenCode blocked, an adversary run landed on claude-sonnet-5. When that happens the cross-family review has become a second self-review, and the verdict is worth less than it looks. Check omp usage, and re-run once the window resets.

Chains changed shape once the per-model allowances were understood

The old design treated an OpenCode failure as a gateway failure, so the next rung was always a different vendor. With per-model allowances that is wrong: one model running out says nothing about the others. On 30 August glm-5.3-flash served requests in the same minute that muse-spark was being refused. So the chains now rotate among the three permitted OpenCode models first, and only then leave for Anthropic.

The setting that hid 96% of the allowance

This one cost most of an afternoon and is the single most useful thing in this document.

OpenCode reports only three aggregate numbers to omp: a 5 hour figure, a weekly figure and a monthly figure. There are no per-model rows in omp usage --json. The per-model allowances exist only on the web dashboard.

With retry.usageAwareFallback: true, omp checks that aggregate before every call and skips models whose provider looks exhausted. Because the monthly aggregate read 100%, omp skipped every OpenCode model, including Muse Spark sitting at 2.2% of its own $60 allowance. Two review runs were pushed onto Sonnet while 97.8% of the intended budget went unused.

# omp/config.yml
retry:
  modelFallback: true
-   usageAwareFallback: true
+   usageAwareFallback: false
  usageReservePct: 15
  usageReservePolicy: auto

The next review reached opencode-go/muse-spark-1.2-contributor and cost $0.0022.

What turning it off costs. When a model really is out of allowance you now eat one failed attempt before the chain moves on, instead of skipping it cleanly. That is exactly what fallback chains are for. Leave it off for as long as the plan meters per model while omp sees only the total.

opencode-zen was removed from every chain

It looked like a free safety net. It is neither free nor a net.

  • It is a second wallet, and it spends real money. zen bills against a workspace spending limit on top of the $10 subscription. A call returned 401 Your workspace has reached its monthly spending limit of $10. Raising that limit to $20 brought zen back and did nothing for Go, whose monthly still read 100%. Two independent ceilings.
  • Its free models do not exist. muse-spark-1.2-contributor-free and deepseek-v4-flash-free return 401 Model is disabled. minimax-m3-free returns 401 ... is not supported.
  • Its catalog cannot serve this fleet. No glm-5.3-flash at all, the newest is glm-5.2. Its Muse Spark is the non-contributor tier at $1.25/$4.25 per Mtok rather than $0.10/$0.20, and both contributor ids are dead.

All three permitted models are available on Go under plan allowance, so there is no reason to reach for cash. zen is now referenced nowhere.

A probe that looks like success. Running omp -p --model <id> against a broken model returns a clean answer, because the fallback chain answers instead and the reply looks identical. Three dead zen ids passed this way before the real errors surfaced. To actually test a route, read error_message in ~/.omp/stats.db after a real subagent run, or curl the provider endpoint directly.

The subscription decision behind all of this

Three plans were on the table on 30 August. Two are staying, one is going.

Claude Max 5x

$100 / month

Drives everything that needs judgment. Sat at 40% of its weekly window with 60% spare, and returned about $1,226 of inference at list price over the month. Best value of the three.

Keep

OpenCode Go

$10 / month

Carries eight roles. The whole month of Muse Spark work came to 2.2% of one allowance. The blowout was never volume, it was pointing $15-allowance models at bulk work.

Keep

Codex Pro 5x

$100 / month

Sat at 12% of its weekly window. Strong models, but nothing in the config needs them once the adversary moves to GLM, and Claude Max has the spare capacity to absorb the rest.

Cancelled

ChatGPT Plus

$20 / month

Cannot do the job. Sol is capped at 10 to 100 messages per 5 hour window on Plus, against 2,805 Sol calls in the previous week. You would hit the wall on day one and fall back to Claude, which you already have.

Not bought

Net effect: $100 a month less, and no role loses its model.

The number that would change this

Claude Max weekly usage. It was at 40% before the Codex work moved across. If it settles above roughly 75%, the $20 becomes worth spending, but as extra Codex capacity rather than as a replacement for OpenCode Go. Check with omp usage.

Two corrections to earlier notes in this repo

Both were wrong in ways that would have led to the wrong fix.

omp's cost numbers are estimates, not the meter

omp stats priced one review at $11.36 on GLM 5.3 and called it a $10 window blown by a single run. The plan's own meter puts GLM 5.3's entire month at 9.4%, which is a fraction of one model's allowance rather than the whole plan. The estimate was not slightly off, it pointed at the wrong model. Use omp stats to find which model and which folder caused load. For how much plan is left, only the per-model dashboard is reliable, and omp usage shows just the capped total.

GLM 5.3 was not the model that burned the month

The first diagnosis blamed GLM 5.3 on the strength of that $11.36 estimate, and moving the reviewer off it was still the right call for cache-read reasons. But it was never the biggest line. GPT 5.6 Luna took 55.1% of the month on a $15 allowance, with Hy3 another 10.3%. Those two are 65% of the window, omp priced Luna at just $2.67, and neither model appears anywhere in omp/config.yml. Whatever spends them needs the same three-model restriction, or the remaining 35% is the most this config can ever have.
