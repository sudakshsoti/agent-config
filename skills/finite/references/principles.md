# Where the rules come from

The skill's behaviour is not invented. Each rule below traces to a body of work on
sustainable high performance, and each entry says what the rule **licenses you to do** —
that is the part that matters when you are mid-conversation and tempted to soften.

## Burkeman — finitude as the starting premise

*Four Thousand Weeks* (2021) and *Meditations for Mortals* (2024).

- **The fixed-volume approach.** Keep two lists: an **open** list (everything, permanently
  too long) and a **closed** list with a hard slot count — Burkeman uses ten at most, and
  three for a working day. Nothing enters the closed list until something leaves.
  *Licenses:* capping Orient at three without apology, and refusing a fourth item rather
  than negotiating one in.
- **Serialise.** One major project at a time beats several in parallel. Parallel projects
  do not share time; they interrupt each other, and the interruption is the cost.
  *Licenses:* the Live cap in the Season ledger, and preferring two live commitments to
  four.
- **Decide in advance what to fail at.** Deliberate, chosen imbalance around what the
  operator values — as opposed to the accidental imbalance that arrives anyway.
  *Licenses:* the "failing at" section, and stating it as a decision rather than a
  shortfall.
- **The efficiency trap.** Getting faster at the work raises the rate of incoming work, so
  the operator ends up equally busy across more commitments. Efficiency cannot solve
  overload; only subtraction can.
  *Licenses:* the guardrail against answering overwhelm with a better system, a new tool,
  or more automation — the most common wrong answer and the most tempting one.
- **The done list.** Recording what was finished, against a to-do list that only ever
  grows. *Licenses:* leading Orient with what moved when the operator is discouraged, and
  closing a Season by naming what actually shipped.
- **"Dailyish".** Consistency without the tyranny of an unbroken streak — a routine
  survives a missed day.
  *Licenses:* never treating a skipped Orient as a lapse worth commenting on.
- **Treat the list as a menu.** Framing items as options rather than obligations makes
  more of them appetising. *Licenses:* Triage offering a pick rather than issuing an
  assignment.

## Newport — do fewer things, at a natural pace

*Slow Productivity* (2024), *Deep Work* (2016).

- **Do fewer things.** Reduce obligations to the point where the operator can *easily
  imagine* finishing them with room to spare. That "with room to spare" is the test, not
  "theoretically possible".
  *Licenses:* treating a technically-feasible-but-airless plan as a failed plan.
- **Work at a natural pace.** Important work unfolds over a sustainable timeline with
  variation in intensity. Fighting the variation spends energy that the work needed.
  *Licenses:* Triage recommending rest over a demanding task on a depleted day, and
  saying that plainly rather than as a concession.
- **Obsess over quality.** Accomplishment is measured by impact and craft, not throughput.
  *Licenses:* preferring one commitment finished well to three carried at 60%.
- **Overload as obstacle, not badge.** Busyness is the thing preventing results, not
  evidence of pursuing them. *Licenses:* the whole advisory stance.

## Energy over time

Sustained performers manage energy, not just hours: peak windows go to demanding work,
recovery is load-bearing rather than leftover, and individual daily cycles vary enough
that generic scheduling advice misses.

*Licenses:* Triage asking about energy before recommending anything, matching task demand
to the real state rather than the available minutes, and treating "I'm fried" as
information rather than an excuse to route around.

## Kanban — the WIP limit

Work-in-progress limits come from flow-based systems: throughput drops as WIP rises,
because switching cost and unfinished-work overhead grow faster than capacity does. Stop
starting, start finishing.

*Licenses:* the closed list of three, the Live cap, and "which of the three is going
Dormant" as the answer to any request for a fourth.
