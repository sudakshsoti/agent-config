# Postures, diagnosis and playbooks

Stance-per-relationship, arena playbooks, backfire signatures. Consult when choosing a
route, when the situation is transactional, or when checking whether a recommended move
has a known failure pattern.

## The ladder, applied

| Rung | Classical | At work | With family or neighbours | With a company or brand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Wait | Let a bad decision meet reality; let a rival overreach in writing | Let an obligation lapse without refusing it | Let the ticket age to the SLA breach that unlocks escalation |
| 1 | Sama | Reframe the ask around their metric; make yes cheaper than no | Agree in principle, defer on logistics | Polite factual restatement with dates and reference numbers |
| 2 | Dana | Trade scope, offer to own something they dislike | Give on a low-cost item to bank refusal capital | Accept a partial remedy in exchange for closing quietly |
| 3 | Bheda | Change who is in the room; build the ally before the meeting; route around the blocker | Route the request through the person who can decline it for you | Go around the support tier to a named human or a public channel |
| 4 | Danda | Borrowed authority: manager, skip-level, policy, process, the written record | Refuse plainly and absorb the cost | Regulatory complaint, public post, chargeback, legal notice |

Rung 4 at work almost always means borrowing authority. Price the loan — it spends the
lender's capital and they will remember the withdrawal.

## Six postures (shadgunya) — a stance per relationship, held at once

The ladder picks a tactic for one counterparty. The postures set a stance toward every
party at once, which is what a real situation requires. Concepts in `sources.md`.

| Posture | Meaning | At work | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sandhi | Accord | Explicit alignment, joint ownership | Constrains later moves against them |
| Vigraha | Open opposition | Contest the decision on the record | Expensive; only where the record matters |
| Asana | Hold position | Neither concede nor escalate; keep working | Free unless the contest is already decided |
| Yana | Prepare to move | Build the record, line up the ally, do not fire | Nearly free, and reversible |
| Samshraya | Shelter under a stronger power | Route through a manager, a sponsor, or policy | Subordination is the price; the win is the lender's |
| Dvaidhibhava | Dual policy | Accord with one party while opposing another | Only holds while the two do not compare notes |

Default combination for most work situations: sandhi with the manager, asana toward the
friction, yana in the background. Escalate one posture at a time, not the whole set.

## Power-base inventory (saptanga) — check before recommending a campaign

Seven limbs, not one quantity: relationship capital, attributed track record, information
access, allies, formal remit, the manager's confidence, time.

- **Do not open a campaign from the weakest limb.** Name which limb the move draws on, and
  whether it is currently strong enough to bear it.
- **Treasury first.** When capital is low — newly joined, newly promoted, a recent
  expensive ask — consolidation beats initiative. Recommending action in a low-treasury
  phase is a misread of the phase, not a bold call.

Assess every undertaking on three terms, not one: **kshaya** (what is lost), **vyaya**
(what is spent), **labha** (what is gained). A move can be affordable and still leave the
user unable to make the next one.

## Testing an ally before betting on them (upadha)

When the read on someone is new or unreliable, the answer is a cheap test, not a confident
guess. Give a small piece of non-damaging information and observe where it travels. Ask
the minor favour before the major one. Watch what they do with credit that is ambiguously
attributable. None of this requires deception — it requires sequencing the small bet
before the large one.

## Diagnosing resistance (prakriti-kopa)

Four types, four different answers. Getting the type wrong is the usual reason a
well-chosen tactic fails.

| Type | Looks like | What works |
|---|---|---|
| Frightened | Delay, deferral, cc-ing upward, "let me check" | Reassurance and cover; make the risk theirs to lose, not theirs to carry |
| Proud | Public correction, re-litigating a settled point | A face-saving exit; let them concede without an audience |
| Greedy | Scope claims, credit capture | A trade; make compliance profitable |
| Enraged | Disproportionate reaction to a small trigger | Time and a private channel; nothing lands while it is hot |

## Constraint on alliance-building

Risk scales with the number of confidants (*Discourses* III.6 — conspiracies fail in
disclosure, not conception). Alliance by shared adjacency is cheap and durable; alliance
requiring coordination and secrecy leaks. If a recommendation needs more than two people
to hold something quietly, it is the wrong recommendation. Count the confidants out loud
before recommending.

## Professional playbook

Written for distributed teams, where the decision often happens in a room, and a timezone,
the user is not in. The pattern holds anywhere the user's account is read rather than
heard.

- **Written record beats a verbal position.** When decisions are made in a timezone the
  user is not awake for, their account is read, not heard. A crisp written summary sent
  before the decision-makers' morning frames the conversation before anyone else is awake
  to frame it differently. Usually the cheapest and most repeatable lever available.
- **Pre-wire before the room, not in it.** One-to-one alignment with the two people who
  matter, before the meeting where the decision happens, is rung 3 and reads as diligence,
  not politics.
- **Escalation costs more than it looks.** In a cost-cutting climate, being the source of
  visible internal friction is priced against the user even when they are right. Prefer
  routes that resolve without a visible conflict event.
- **"Let me check", silence, or enthusiasm without a date, all mean no.** Treat it as a
  refusal already delivered and choose the rung accordingly, rather than waiting for an
  explicit one.
- **Credit disputes: contest the record, not the person.** Restate the timeline factually
  in a channel the people whose opinion matters will read, without naming the taker.

## Consumer and transactional playbook

Escalation order, generic: support ticket with a reference number → a named senior contact
or the published grievance officer → a public channel → the sector regulator or ombudsman
→ legal notice.

- Most regulated sectors publish a nodal or grievance officer by mandate. Naming that
  office in a message frequently resolves the matter one tier earlier. In India this is
  near-universal across banking, insurance, telecom and e-commerce.
- Public visibility is rung 3, not rung 4 — it changes who is in the room (the brand's
  social team) rather than applying force.
- Documentation before escalation: dates, reference numbers, screenshots, amounts.
  Escalating without a record collapses at the first tier.
- Hand off the moment it becomes a consumer complaint, an insurance dispute, or a legal
  notice. This skill chooses whether to escalate; a legal specialist executes the route.
- Contractors, building management, residents' associations: precedent governs. Ask what
  was done for the last person who complained, and anchor to that rather than to the rule.

## Arm's-length personal playbook

- Obligation is routed through intermediaries specifically to make direct refusal
  expensive. The counter is to answer the intermediary, not the principal — declining to
  the messenger costs far less than declining to the source.
- Deferral outperforms refusal. Most obligations expire if not refused, because the asker
  moves on to an easier target.
- Gatherings are the enforcement venue. Anything the user wants to refuse should be
  refused before the gathering, in writing or by phone, never in the room.
- Never spend real capital on a low-stakes obligation. Track what is being banked and for
  what — the point of small concessions is a future refusal that will actually cost
  something.

## Backfire signatures — the earliest observable signs

| Move | Backfire | First sign to watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-wiring allies | Reads as canvassing | Someone mentions "I heard you'd already spoken to X" |
| Escalating to the skip-level | The manager treats it as a loss of trust | The manager routes future items around the user, or 1:1s get shorter |
| Building a written record | Reads as building a case | Replies become carefully worded and cc'd |
| Public escalation with a brand | Support closes ranks, the ticket freezes | The reply switches from a named agent to a template |
| Refusing an obligation | The cost lands on someone else in the family instead | The next request arrives through a different family member |
| Waiting | The window closes | The decision appears in someone else's document |
| Waiting while a contest is already decided | Despised by the loser, unrespected by the winner | Both sides stop bringing things to the user |
| Winning via borrowed authority | The room credits the lender | "So-and-so sorted it" in someone else's recap |
| Touching credit or scope | Permanent enemy, non-recoverable | They start documenting their own contributions defensively |

## Why the hard rules are hard

- **Credit and scope.** They are the property and honour of an office, and touching either
  produces hatred rather than opposition — a different and more permanent thing. Almost no
  gain justifies it, and the damage does not decay.
- **Harm in one stroke.** Pushback split across three escalating conversations reads as a
  campaign and is resented for as long as it continues. The same content delivered once
  reads as a decision and is resented briefly.
- **Borrowed authority.** It costs most when it works, because the room records that the
  lender resolved it. Where influence is the quantity being assessed, repeated use
  demonstrates that the user requires an intermediary.
- **Open challenge over backchannel.** On the record strengthens the user's position;
  privately complained about corrodes it and rebounds on the complainant.
- **Alliances in calm weather.** They are only cheap before they are needed. Built under
  pressure, they are visibly transactional and priced accordingly.
