# Channels, authenticity and arbitrage

Authenticity first, price second. This file is the seller-side reference.

## General marketplaces

- **Amazon India** — safe when the listing says "sold by Amazon" or by a named brand-
  authorised seller. A third-party seller on anything above ₹5,000 is a real risk.
- **Flipkart** — same rule; check the seller name and rating, not just the badge.
- **Brand websites direct** — the cleanest path for warranty, usually not the cheapest.
- **Tata CLiQ Luxury** — authorised stock with Indian warranty across beauty, fragrance,
  watches and fashion.
- **Nykaa, Nykaa Luxe** — beauty and fragrance, authorised.
- **Ajio, Myntra** — broad fashion inventory; filter aggressively, check the seller.
- **Croma, Reliance Digital, Vijay Sales** — offline electronics; useful for price
  matching and immediate service escalation.

Assume counterfeit for any branded item on Snapdeal, Meesho or IndiaMart.

## Fashion and menswear

Uniqlo India (the U line is the best value in its lane), Muji (muji.in and Ajio; physical
stores only in Mumbai and Noida), Ajio for fabric-specific filtering, Myntra for breadth,
Andamen for tailored shirts and chinos, M&S for knitwear, Massimo Dutti on sale only,
Nicobar and Fabindia for Indian linens, plus brand sites and Delhi NCR offline stores.

Typical budget bands for mid-market menswear, sale prices included: tops ₹1,500–3,000,
trousers ₹2,000–4,000, footwear ₹4,000–8,000, outerwear ₹5,000–10,000. Aspirational
labels sit outside this and function as quality benchmarks, not buy targets.

Also check Indian versus international sizing, fabric quality, the return policy, and
whether the seller is the brand or a reseller.

## Fragrance

**Authorised:** Sephora India (metro stores, nationwide online), Tata CLiQ Luxury,
Shoppers Stop and Lifestyle, brand boutiques (DLF Emporio in Delhi and the Mumbai and
Bengaluru equivalents), Amazon India only when sold by Amazon or a brand-authorised
seller, Nykaa Luxe, Perfume Booth. Scentido and Parfumdreams ship internationally with a
reasonable reputation — verify their current state before recommending.

**Decants and splits:** Perfume Booth, The Scent Split, Surrender to Chance (US, ships to
India, expensive postage but legitimate), and the Indian Instagram decanter community —
vet each seller per transaction. Push a decant before any full bottle above ₹8,000.

## Counterfeit tells

- "First copy", "original", "1:1 copy", "premium replica", "duty paid duplicate" — all
  mean counterfeit. "Tester" and "unboxed" do not; those can be legitimate.
- A mall kiosk offering a name brand at 60% off is refilled or fake.
- A price far below every authorised channel is the tell, not the bargain.
- **Batch codes** — genuine bottles carry a stamped or printed code on the base and the
  box. CheckFresh and CheckCosmetic decode the production date for major houses. A seller
  who will not send a clear photo of the batch code is selling a fake. A citrus-led
  composition more than five years old will have oxidised even if genuine.
- For electronics accessories, supplements and beauty, apply the same logic: authorised
  seller, sealed packaging, verifiable serial or batch.

## Grey market and duty-free arbitrage

Grey-market stock can be genuine but carries no Indian warranty and no service recourse.
Say that plainly before recommending it, and fold customs and shipping into the INR
comparison.

Dubai duty-free, when the user is travelling via DXB:

- Amouage, Roja, Xerjoff, Initio — typically 30–50% below Indian MRP.
- MFK, Creed — 15–30% below European RRP, far below India.
- Mainstream designer (Dior, Chanel, Hermès) — 10–15%, not worth planning around.
- Customs: personal effects within reasonable limits are fine; several sealed bottles
  above roughly ₹50,000 declared value invite scrutiny.

The same arbitrage logic applies beyond fragrance — electronics bought abroad usually
lose the Indian warranty, which is often worth more than the saving.
