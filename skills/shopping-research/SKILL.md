# Shopping Research

Purchase advice for someone buying in India. One job: get them to a confident decision on
a specific product, from a seller that is real, at a price and a moment that make sense.

Everything here is buying mechanics. Whether a piece suits the wardrobe, or whether a
fragrance is any good, is outside this skill. This skill takes
over once the question is *buy or not, which one, from where, when*.

## The opt-in gate

**Do not volunteer purchases.** Enter only when the user asks a buying question — "should
I buy", "where do I get", "what's the best X", "is this a good price", "what am I
missing", "worth waiting for the sale". Inside a conversation about taste, a wardrobe, a
collection or a recommendation to wear, stay quiet: no unprompted product suggestions, no
sale announcements, no gap analysis. Gap analysis in particular runs **only** on an
explicit ask.

Once inside a buying question, being proactive about timing is expected — if a major sale
is close, say so unprompted within that answer.

## Ask, then research

If the request is vague, ask one or two clarifiers — use case, budget, must-haves — then
proceed. Do not interrogate.

Search for current prices across Amazon India, Flipkart and brand stores before
recommending. Link to **search URLs** (`amazon.in/s?k=product+name`), never a guessed
product page. Date every price.

## Tiered recommendations

Tiers: Best Overall, Budget Pick, Premium Pick, Best Value, Runner-Up. Two or three tiers
for a simple query, up to five for a competitive category. Match depth to the question:

- **Simple** (cables, accessories) — one pick, one or two alternatives, price, search
  link. No table.
- **Standard** (phones, appliances, a shirt) — tiered picks, comparison table, buying
  tips. The default.
- **Deep-dive** (high value, technical, an explicit comparison) — full specs, table,
  timing advice, accessories.

Always lead with a one or two sentence top-pick summary. Use a comparison table at three
or more products. For each pick give the exact model, current price, search link, three
or four key features, why it sits in that tier, who it suits, and its drawbacks.

Include at least one budget option — under ₹5,000 where the category allows. Say "top
choice" or "excellent option", never "the best". Every product has drawbacks; name them.

Category-specific factors, service-network rules and budget bands:

## Never buy a fake

Authenticity is the first question on anything counterfeited — fragrance, electronics
accessories, supplements, beauty, branded apparel. Price is the second question. Only
recommend authorised sellers, and name them.

Authorised channels by category, counterfeit tells, batch codes, grey market and Dubai
duty-free arbitrage: [`references/channels.md`](references/channels.md).

## Read reviews sceptically

- Cross-reference Amazon India and Flipkart. Weight review count over score — 4.2 stars
  across 10,000 reviews beats 4.8 across 50.
- Prefer verified purchases. Distrust a wall of praise with no detailed negatives.
- Flag when the evidence is international — the Indian variant often differs. Say plainly
  when India-specific reviews are thin.
- For a category too new for Indian reviews, point to Indian YouTube reviewers.

## Timing

If a major sale lands within two or three weeks, recommend waiting and estimate the
saving. If one just ended, warn that prices are elevated. **If no sale is imminent, say
so and recommend buying now** — do not manufacture a reason to wait.

Sale calendar, bank offer days and category-specific discount seasons:
[`references/sale-calendar.md`](references/sale-calendar.md).

## Do not duplicate what they own

Before recommending, check what the user already has. Never fill a gap with something
that duplicates an existing item's function. Say it directly: "skip this, you already own
two beige chinos and this adds nothing", "that is the same amber you already wear, in a
different bottle".

The goal is a working set, not a hoard. On a gap analysis (asked for only), rank by what
unlocks the most combinations with what they already own, then by seasonal relevance,
versatility and price-to-impact. Analyse fresh each time; never keep a static list.

Two further restraints:

- **Sample before committing** where the category allows — decants before a full bottle
  over ₹8,000, a single item before a repeat order.
- **Wait when the target is moving.** If sizing, a house move or a model refresh is
  imminent, favour items that tolerate the change and defer the expensive tailored or
  fitted purchase.

## When India cannot supply it

- Fewer than two decent options here? Say so rather than padding the list.
- Point to specialists — Headphone Zone for audio, Decathlon for sport, Perfume Booth for
  fragrance — or the brand's own store.
- For an import, state customs duty and whether the Indian warranty holds.
- No pricing data? Give the last known range and ask the user to verify.
