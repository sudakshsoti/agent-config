# Visual essay

## Fit and job

Use for evidence-led arguments, comparisons and complex explanations where visual evidence advances a sequence of ideas. This is a narrative treatment within the artifact's classified job, not permission to turn a requested tool into an essay. Compared with technical field guide, the sequence develops an argument; the visuals answer successive questions rather than primarily documenting reusable parts of a mechanism.

These defaults authorise visual choices within the requested content and behaviour; they do not authorise new features or changes to the artifact's job.

## Defaults

- Composition: connect each section's point to the visual evidence that supports it. Let the question determine the chart, diagram, annotation or comparison. Build a clear sequence from the supplied material; do not manufacture a thesis or evidence.
- Density: alternate explanation and evidence as comprehension requires. Give complex visuals sufficient room while keeping the relevant claim, legend and annotation close enough to read together.
- Typography roles: use a readable narrative voice with clear section headings, chart labels, annotations, captions and source notes. Visual labels must work at their rendered size rather than inherit a tiny metadata style.
- Palette roles: give categories, quantities and emphasis stable meanings across the essay. Use colour to direct attention to evidence; distinguish highlighted and contextual information without making the latter unreadable.
- Grouping and surfaces: integrate visuals with prose in the page flow. Use framing only when it clarifies a comparison, separates a dataset or supports the visual itself. Avoid turning the sequence into interchangeable dashboard cards.
- Interaction: use requested interaction when manipulating or revealing evidence improves understanding. Keep the basic explanation available without requiring animation, hover or precise gestures. Scroll position may support progression, but must not be the only way to understand the content.

## Three distinguishing features

1. Each main visual answers an identifiable question raised by the surrounding text.
2. The sequence changes what the reader understands, instead of repeating the same chart-and-card layout.
3. Claims, annotations, units and sources are presented with the evidence they qualify.

## Permitted variation

The essay may be static, lightly interactive or include requested stepwise visual changes. A visual can span more width than the prose when that improves comprehension. Different sections may need different graphic forms; retain consistent data meanings and reading roles across them. Use supplied evidence and label uncertainty or illustrative assumptions rather than inventing measurements.

## Avoid

- Dashboard summary cards substituted for an argument.
- A different chart type in every section merely to create variety.
- Scroll hijacking, mandatory animation or hover-only explanations.
- Unsupported claims, decorative numbers, misleading scales or colour meanings that change between figures.

## Visible review criteria

- Every main visual advances the supplied explanation or argument and has readable labels, units and qualifications.
- The narrow layout keeps the relevant text and visual evidence in a comprehensible order.
- Colour and chart encodings keep consistent meanings across sections.
- The core explanation remains understandable with reduced motion and without hover; requested controls are keyboard accessible.
