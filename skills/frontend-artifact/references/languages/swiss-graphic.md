# Swiss graphic

## Fit and job

Use for principles, taxonomies, conceptual artifacts and structured visual systems when composition can make relationships legible. The character comes from a strong grid, deliberate scale contrast and hard alignment. Compared with precision software, the grid communicates the subject's structure rather than primarily organising an operational interface.

These defaults authorise visual choices within the requested content and behaviour; they do not authorise new features or changes to the artifact's job.

## Defaults

- Composition: choose a small set of shared alignment lines and make headings, text and graphic elements relate to them. Express genuine hierarchy or classification through position and scale. The grid is a layout system, not a decorative background.
- Density: place closely related material together and use larger spaces to separate conceptual levels. Keep comparison groups visible together where practical; avoid equal spacing that disguises their relationships.
- Typography roles: use a disciplined type system with contrasting scale or weight where the content hierarchy warrants it. Select the actual family and spacing through proofing; do not apply tight tracking because the direction is Swiss. Keep supporting text readable.
- Palette roles: use a restrained base with a deliberate accent or a limited set of colours tied to classification. Maintain semantic status colours if the artifact contains controls or data.
- Grouping and surfaces: prefer hard alignment, rules, blocks of meaningful colour and open space over rounded cards and shadows. A containing shape should identify a real group or distinction.
- Interaction: keep behaviour direct and legible. If requested interaction transforms or filters the composition, preserve orientation and relationships; do not add motion as a graphic flourish alone.

## Three distinguishing features

1. Major elements share an evident grid rather than independent centred containers.
2. Differences in scale and position correspond to specific levels or relationships in the content.
3. Graphic marks and colour blocks carry classification or emphasis, with no ornamental grid behind them.

## Permitted variation

The grid may be asymmetric or balanced. Display scale is available for presentation-like or editorial reading when it helps the idea; it cannot displace task content on a utility. Colour can be bold or restrained. On narrow screens, change the number of columns and preserve the conceptual grouping rather than shrinking a desktop poster.

## Avoid

- Treating a giant heading, all-caps labels and negative tracking as sufficient direction.
- Decorative numbering, rules or coloured shapes that imply nonexistent relationships.
- Poster composition that breaks reading order or hides the primary task on a phone.
- Refusing necessary accessible control boundaries because the visual language favours open space.

## Visible review criteria

- The reader can infer the main hierarchy and grouping from alignment and scale without relying on explanatory decoration.
- Every prominent number, rule and colour block refers to real content or an explicit emphasis.
- The narrow layout preserves relationships and reading order rather than reproducing a miniature poster.
- The primary task remains visible within the classified surface's viewport target.
