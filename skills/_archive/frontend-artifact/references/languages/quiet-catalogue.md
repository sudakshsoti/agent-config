# Quiet catalogue

## Fit and job

Use for research collections, portfolios, cultural subjects and considered browsing of objects or ideas. The character comes from restrained hierarchy, consistent entry treatment and little interface chrome. Compared with editorial modernist, the primary structure helps readers browse or compare a collection rather than follow a sustained argument.

These defaults authorise visual choices within the requested content and behaviour; they do not authorise new features or changes to the artifact's job.

## Defaults

- Composition: let the actual entries or topics establish the page. Give repeated metadata a consistent order so readers can compare without relearning each item. Use imagery when it helps identify or understand the subject; text-only collections are valid.
- Density: allow space for considered inspection while keeping related title, image and metadata together. Set density from the browsing task; generous spacing must not prevent useful comparison or delay a reference lookup.
- Typography roles: use restrained contrast between entry titles, descriptive text and metadata. Select a reading face with character appropriate to the subject; a serif is optional. Keep supporting text fully legible rather than using faint colour as the main hierarchy tool.
- Palette roles: begin with warm or softly neutral surfaces and a clear text contrast. Give accent colour a limited role in links, selection or useful emphasis. Let meaningful object imagery retain its own colour relationships.
- Grouping and surfaces: prefer open groups, rules, alignment and changes of spacing over repeated bordered cards. Keep navigation and controls visually restrained but recognisable.
- Interaction: make ordinary links, selection and requested browsing controls predictable. Feedback should be clear without drawing attention away from the collection. Do not introduce elaborate reveals or transitions to imply refinement.

## Three distinguishing features

1. Entries or topics lead the page and share a consistent metadata order.
2. Restraint comes from limited visual competition and minimal chrome, not weak contrast or missing labels.
3. Space separates objects or ideas while keeping each entry's own parts together.

## Permitted variation

Lists, rows or grids may fit different collections; choose from the content and comparison task. The palette can be cooler or dark when the subject or existing system requires it. Image size and entry density can vary with the material, while shared reading roles and metadata order preserve coherence. Tactile character comes from actual imagery and type choices; simulated paper texture is not required.

## Avoid

- Cream, brown and a serif applied as a substitute for a considered collection structure.
- Empty space that leaves the first useful entry below a decorative introduction.
- Low-contrast metadata, hidden controls or oversized imagery that prevents comparison.
- Invented objects, photographs or descriptions added to imitate a catalogue reference.

## Visible review criteria

- The collection's entries or topics are easy to locate, and reference or utility uses meet their first-viewport target.
- Metadata appears in a consistent order and remains readable at the narrowest target width.
- Whitespace separates meaningful groups without detaching titles, images and their descriptions.
- Links and controls remain visibly identifiable in an otherwise restrained interface.
