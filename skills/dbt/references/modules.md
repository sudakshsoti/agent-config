# Skills Modules — Quick Reference

Lookup tables for the four DBT skills modules. The teaching content for modules one to
three is in [`framework.md`](framework.md) §5–§7; this file is the at-a-glance table plus
module four, which `framework.md` names but does not number. Cite the §N anchors below
when coaching or when anchoring a worksheet.

## The four modules

| Module | Anchor | What it is for | Core skills |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core mindfulness | §5 | Foundation for everything else; observing internal states without being swept away | Wise Mind, the What skills, the How skills |
| Distress tolerance | §6 | Surviving crisis without making it worse; accepting what cannot change now | STOP, TIP, ACCEPTS, Self-Soothe, IMPROVE, radical acceptance |
| Emotion regulation | §7 | Naming emotions, lowering vulnerability, changing unwanted emotions | Check the Facts, Opposite Action, ABC PLEASE, cope ahead |
| Interpersonal effectiveness | this file | Asking, refusing, and keeping the relationship and self-respect intact | DEAR MAN, GIVE, FAST |

Teaching order for a beginner: mindfulness first, then distress tolerance when distress is
live, then emotion regulation, then interpersonal effectiveness. One skill, rehearsed,
before the next. Do not hand over the whole system at once.

## Mnemonics

| Mnemonic | Module | Expansion |
|---|---|---|
| **STOP** | §6.1 | Stop · Take a step back · Observe · Proceed mindfully |
| **TIP** | §6.1 | Temperature · Intense exercise · Paced breathing · Paired muscle relaxation |
| **ACCEPTS** | §6.1 | Activities · Contributing · Comparisons · Emotions · Pushing away · Thoughts · Sensations |
| **IMPROVE** | §6.1 | Imagery · Meaning · Prayer · Relaxation · One thing at a time · Vacation · Encouragement |
| **ABC PLEASE** | §7.3 | Accumulate positives · Build mastery · Cope ahead + treat PhysicaL illness · balance Eating · avoid mood-Altering substances · balance Sleep · get Exercise |
| **DEAR MAN** | below | Describe · Express · Assert · Reinforce · stay Mindful · Appear confident · Negotiate |
| **GIVE** | below | Gentle · Interested · Validate · Easy manner |
| **FAST** | below | Fair · no Apologies · Stick to values · Truthful |

Two cautions carried from §6.1 and §7.3, both easy to miss:

- **TIP temperature.** The cold-face dive reflex drops heart rate. Check for cardiac or
  eating-disorder history before recommending it. In someone with health anxiety, the
  sensation spike can feed a catastrophic body-interpretation loop — frame the sensation as
  the skill working before use, and pair it with Check the Facts (§7.2) on the body reading.
- **PLEASE "treat physical illness."** In health anxiety this can turn into a
  body-monitoring trap. Set what "treating it" means in advance, in behavioural terms.

## Module Four — Interpersonal Effectiveness

Standard Linehan module, summarised here because `framework.md` does not carry it as a
numbered section. Three goals, three mnemonics, chosen by which goal matters most in the
situation. Naming the priority first is the skill; running all three at once is not.

- **Objective effectiveness — DEAR MAN.** Getting what you asked for.
  **D**escribe the facts, **E**xpress how you feel about them, **A**ssert the ask or the
  refusal plainly, **R**einforce by naming what the other person gets. Stay **M**indful of
  the ask (the broken-record move: repeat it rather than get pulled off track), **A**ppear
  confident in voice and posture, **N**egotiate — offer an alternative, ask for one.
- **Relationship effectiveness — GIVE.** Keeping the relationship intact through the ask.
  Be **G**entle (no attacks, threats or judging), act **I**nterested, **V**alidate what the
  other person feels or wants (§9), use an **E**asy manner — some lightness.
- **Self-respect effectiveness — FAST.** Coming out of it still liking yourself.
  Be **F**air to both sides, no unnecessary **A**pologies, **S**tick to your own values,
  be **T**ruthful — no exaggerating, no excuses.

Intensity is a dial, not a switch: how hard to ask or how firmly to refuse is set by
capability, priorities, the relationship, the other person's rights, and timing. Cope ahead
(§7.3) is the rehearsal step before a hard conversation, and a DEAR MAN written out in
advance is the standard homework for one.
