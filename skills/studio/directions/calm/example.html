Play from large flat colour fields and 24 to 112px radii. The warm ground and the warm near-black ink are what stop it reading as childish.

Measured against headspace.com. One family, three weights.

What it does

One file, one direction, proved by rendering it

studio builds one self-contained HTML artifact against a named design direction, renders it at 390 and 1440, scores it, and revises once before handing it over.

There is no default direction. The choice is made before any markup exists, because the direction is what the artifact gets judged against: each one carries its own avoid list, and that list is worth 60 of the 100 points at the gate. The remaining 40 are the universal rubric every direction shares, which is also why the universal half alone would make everything average.

This page is the calm direction rendering a brief that the other three render as well. Set them beside each other and the only thing that changes is the direction.

Registry

Four directions

Each one is measured against a named reference rather than invented, and each one states in words what it must not do.

editorial

Ground: light paper

Two families, no negative tracking, almost no radius.

Suits: long reads, essays, explainers, reports

instrument

Ground: white

One grotesque, one accent, bordered cards, zero shadow.

Suits: pricing, comparisons, technical product pages

product-dark

Ground: near-black

Alpha-tint elevation, white-alpha hairlines, tight tracking.

Suits: developer tools, dashboards-as-marketing

calm this page

Ground: warm off-white

One family, large flat colour fields, large radii.

Suits: consumer, wellbeing, onboarding, anything friendly

Phase 0

Fonts are a blacklist, not a whitelist

Twenty families are banned outright. Anything not on that list is available, and the list only ever grows.

Inter carries body and UI, never display. Inter at 72px in a hero is the loudest signal that an agent made the page. Across the measured references, family count is one or two and never more, and the display weight is rarely bold.

This direction is single-family throughout, which is what the reference does: Hanken Grotesk sets the display, the body and the UI, with IBM Plex Mono reserved for literal commands.

Closing note

The signature move

A large flat colour field at a 112px radius, sitting on warm off-white, with warm near-black ink for every word that is not on the field.

The play is entirely in the field: its size, its flatness, its radius. No gradient, no shadow, no border, and nothing inside it but words. Three fields is the ceiling for one artifact, and this page spends all three: the masthead, the font policy, and this.

What stops it reading as childish is the pair underneath. The ground is warm off-white rather than white, and the ink is warm near-black rather than black. Swap either one for a neutral and the same radii, the same layout and the same colours turn into a children's app.

Specimen · spacing

Eight steps on a 4px grid

Each bar is exactly one step wide; judge whether the scale widens the way the radius scale widens, small increments where things touch and large jumps where they separate.

--space-1 · 0.25rem, 4px — inside a badge, the focus-ring offset, icon to text.

--space-2 · 0.5rem, 8px — a label to its value.

--space-3 · 0.75rem, 12px — between stacked lines of metadata.

--space-4 · 1rem, 16px — paragraph spacing, and the gutter at 390.

--space-5 · 1.5rem, 24px — card padding at 390, grid gap.

--space-6 · 2.5rem, 40px — card padding at 1440, the space under a heading block.

--space-7 · 4rem, 64px — padding inside a colour field, section break at 390.

--space-8 · 6rem, 96px — between sections at 1440, and the page's top and bottom air.

The rhythm is deliberately uneven: prose sits at 12px, section breaks at 64 or 96. calm declares no rail token, so the page's own 1120px container carries the layout measure and the eight steps are rhythm only.

Specimen · radius

Four corners, and the gaps between them

The three small steps first, then the signature step at the only width it is specified for; judge whether 8, 24 and 112 read as three unmistakably different shapes rather than as everything being a bit rounded.

--radius-sm · 8px — code, badges, inputs.

--radius-md · 24px — cards and panels.

--radius-pill · 9999px — buttons.

--radius-lg · clamp(40px, 8vw, 112px) — colour fields only.

Small holds anything with a literal in it. Medium is the characteristic card corner, and the one-sided 24px 24px 0 0 the font-policy field uses above is written from it. The pill is a true capsule at any height, where the measured 800px stops being one on a genuinely tall element. The large step is 112px wherever there is room and eases to 40px on a phone, because a flat 112 there would take 224 of about 358 available pixels and turn the slab into a lozenge.

The large step is shown at full width because that is the only place the token is legal: under the measure the same corner reads as decoration rather than as paint. It is painted in --field-blue, which holds the same value as the masthead's --accent, so the specimen shows the signature without spending a fourth hue. No radius token here is 0 — the jump between the steps is the whole idea.

Specimen · elevation

One blur, one press, and tone for everything else

calm is the one direction of the four that signals depth with blur, because the reference measurably does: of the seven references only Mercury and Headspace blur anything at all.

--ground — flat. Nothing sits behind the page, and the hairline is --hair, there only so the tile has an edge to be seen by.

--surface-raised with --shadow-1 · 0 1px 8px oklch(0.188 0.002 17 / 0.2) — the measured Headspace card lift, a 1px offset at 20%. This is the whole depth budget.

--surface-sunk, no shadow — a recess for a literal rather than for prose. Depth by tone, which is how the rest of the page does it.

--shadow-press · 0 2px 0 oklch(0.3670 0.0145 308 / 0.2) — an affordance with zero blur, not a second depth level. It belongs on a button and nowhere else; see the buttons under States.

Colour fields are painted, not raised, so they carry no shadow at any size. That rule is what keeps the page from reading as a stack of coloured cards, and it is what makes the one blurred shadow mean something when it appears.
